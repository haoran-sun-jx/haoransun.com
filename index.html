Haoran Sun

Researcher, Durham University Business School

My research combines economic theory and applied microeconometrics to understand how government intervention shapes competition, innovation, and productivity. On the theoretical side, I develop game-theoretic models of government intervention, including subsidies and taxation, in markets characterised by strategic competition and network externalities that make demand self-reinforcing. On the empirical side, I conduct causal analysis of the firm-level effects of innovation support, external advice, and AI adoption on productivity using ONS microdata (Accredited Researcher, 2019–present), alongside regional analysis of investment patterns and high-growth business dynamics using firm-level data.

Job Market Paper

Job Market Paper
Strategic Autonomy vs. Race to the Bottom: Self-Defeating Industrial Policy in the Presence of Network Externalities
Nigar Hashimzade and Haoran Sun
This paper develops a two-country model to examine the strategic trade-off between R&D subsidies and foreign-firm taxation in sectors where network externalities make market dominance self-reinforcing. The central result is a “carrot-and-stick” equilibrium: the optimal policy combines an R&D subsidy for the domestic firm with a tax on the foreign rival, and this policy mix arises specifically because network externalities create demand feedback that makes the two instruments complementary. The model shows that optimal support is increasing in the strength of network externalities and that misdirected support in self-reinforcing markets can permanently cede an industry to a competitor.
